Canada PAL or TAL: when an applicant may need one

Provincial and territorial attestation letter rules depend on the application and the applicant’s situation. Check IRCC’s current decision guidance before submitting.

Draft author: Eduforn Editorial TeamUpdated October 4, 2026 · reviewer to be assigned

What is a PAL or TAL?

A provincial attestation letter (PAL) or territorial attestation letter (TAL) is issued for study permit applications under Canada’s current process. IRCC says the letter is provided by the province or territory where you plan to study, through the designated learning institution you plan to attend. It generally confirms that your application is included in the province or territory’s allocation.

Do all applicants need one?

No single short checklist safely covers every applicant. IRCC sets out exceptions for certain schools, programs and situations. The list includes specific primary and secondary studies, some graduate programs at public designated learning institutions, certain exchange students and other described cases. Quebec has its own certificate process: most applicants who do not need a PAL or TAL and plan to study in Quebec still need a Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ), subject to limited exceptions.

These details can change and depend on facts such as where you apply, your program and status. Use IRCC’s current “find out if you need a PAL/TAL” questions, then review the full official list of exceptions. If an exception appears to apply, IRCC may still require evidence that you meet it.

How to prepare

  • Confirm the designated institution and exact program with the school.
  • Ask the institution about its current process for issuing the provincial or territorial letter.
  • Check whether the letter must be included with your permit application and that it is valid for the current cap year.
  • For Quebec, ask the institution about the CAQ process and check the Government of Quebec instructions.
  • Review IRCC’s complete application checklist immediately before submitting.

Eduforn can help students understand application steps, but IRCC determines what is required for an individual application. A PAL/TAL or CAQ is not a permit approval.
