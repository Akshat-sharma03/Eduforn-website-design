IELTS vs PTE for studying abroad: how to choose

There is no single best English test for every student. The right choice is the test and version accepted by your exact institution, course and, where relevant, visa route.

Draft author: Eduforn Editorial TeamUpdated October 4, 2026 · reviewer to be assigned

Start with the requirement, not the test brand

Before paying for a test, open the current admissions page for every course on your shortlist. Record which test versions are accepted, the minimum overall result, any minimum component results, score validity requirements and the date results must arrive. A university may accept several tests, while a particular course, professional program or pathway may set a narrower rule.

Compare format and test conditions

IELTS Academic and PTE Academic assess English skills in different task formats. Review the official sample tasks and test-day information for each one. Consider whether you prefer the question styles, how you perform when speaking to a person or recording a response, local test availability, accessibility arrangements, delivery format, and the time you can devote to preparation. Do not choose from a general claim that one test is easier.

Be precise about the test name. An academic admissions test, a version used for a particular visa route, and other test products are not automatically interchangeable. If a government route requires a specific approved test, check the current government list as well as the institution’s requirement.

Make a simple comparison

  • Write down each institution’s accepted test and version.
  • Compare required scores for every section, not only the overall result.
  • Check score validity and the last date results can be submitted.
  • Compare locations, available dates, fees, retake policies and official preparation resources.
  • Choose after you have verified requirements for your shortlist.

Eduforn lists IELTS, PTE and SELT preparation. Ask the team which training options are currently available. The institution or authority decides whether a score meets its requirements.
